PLAIN-ENGLISH GUIDE

What this app is actually doing

You do not need to understand property-investment jargon. The app has two jobs: first find markets worth investigating, then test whether a particular property makes financial sense.

1

Find a promising market

The Market Finder asks: “Which suburbs show signs of strong demand, limited rental supply and reasonable prices?” It creates a shortlist. A high score means investigate this suburb, not buy anything in it.

2

Analyse an actual property

Enter the asking price, expected rent, deposit, loan rate and real running costs. The app then works out yield, cash required, loan repayments and expected monthly cash flow.

3

Check the land and planning risks

The planning lookup screens for zoning, minimum lot size, flood or inundation, heritage and bushfire information. These checks can uncover both problems and value-add opportunities such as a large developable block.

4

Verify the assumptions

Replace estimates with actual figures: a rental appraisal, council rates, insurance quote, finance quote and—before buying—professional inspections and planning advice. The closer the inputs are to reality, the more useful the result becomes.

MARKET FINDER

How to read the suburb numbers

Demand to Supply Ratio What is it?

A score designed to compare buyer demand with the amount of property available. In the supplied spreadsheet, 50 or more is the initial screening target.

HigherMore demand relative to supplyLowerLess compelling for this strategy

It is a screening metric from the supplied data source, not a guarantee that prices will rise.

Vacancy rate

The percentage of rental properties currently sitting vacant. For a landlord, lower is generally better because there are fewer empty rentals competing for tenants.

Around 0–2%Tight rental market2–2.8%Still within our current screenAbove 2.8%Needs more scrutiny

Gross rental yield

Annual rent divided by property price. It tells you how much rent the property produces relative to what it costs, before expenses.

Weekly rent × 52 ÷ purchase price

Example: $405 a week on a $285,000 property is about 7.4% gross yield.

Typical value

A suburb-level typical property value. We use it mainly to keep the search inside your buying range. It is not a valuation of the property you are considering.

Market Score

Our own screening score out of 100. It combines the market data available in the supplied spreadsheets. It is deliberately not presented as an industry rating or prediction.

75–100 Strong shortlist55–74 Worth investigatingBelow 55 Less attractive on current rules

Extra market metrics

The companion spreadsheet also wants measures such as stock on market, days on market, price growth and building approvals. The app only uses them when real values are available; missing data is never silently treated as zero.

PROPERTY ANALYSER

How to read the deal numbers

Cash required

The amount you need to get into the deal: deposit plus purchase costs that are not being financed. This matters because two properties with similar returns can require very different amounts of your own money.

Loan-to-Value Ratio

The loan compared with the property's value. An 80% Loan-to-Value Ratio means the lender funds 80% and you provide 20% before purchase costs. Higher ratios can increase risk and may trigger Lenders Mortgage Insurance.

Lenders Mortgage Insurance

An insurance premium that protects the lender, not you, if the loan is high relative to the property's value. It can sometimes be added to the loan instead of paid entirely upfront.

Interest Only versus Principal and Interest

Interest Only keeps repayments lower but does not reduce the loan balance during that period. Principal and Interest costs more each month because part of each payment reduces the debt.

Net cash flow

What is left after rent minus loan repayments, vacancy allowance, management, rates, water, insurance, maintenance and other entered costs.

PositiveThe property is estimated to put cash in your pocketNegativeYou are estimated to contribute money to hold it

Cash-on-cash return

Annual cash flow compared with the cash you put into the purchase. It helps compare a cheap, highly leveraged deal with a more expensive deal requiring much more of your capital.

Property Score

Our screening score for the individual property. It considers the financial result plus value-add, development and risk inputs. It is not a valuation and should never replace due diligence.

Opportunity Score

The combined view of market quality + the individual property. A great property in a weak market and a poor property in a great market should not look identical.

PLANNING

How to interpret the government checks

Zoning

Tells you broadly what the land can be used for. It is one of the first clues when investigating a second dwelling, subdivision or another value-add idea.

Minimum lot size

The mapped minimum size for certain subdivision outcomes where the relevant planning rules apply. A block being larger than this number does not automatically mean it can be subdivided.

Flood / inundation

Found means a relevant government spatial layer intersects the property location. That can affect insurance, development and resale. Unknown means the app could not establish the answer—it does not mean safe.

Heritage and bushfire

These overlays can add building, development, insurance or approval constraints. They are reasons to investigate further rather than automatic rejection rules.

WORKED EXAMPLE

Reading 1 Clarke Street, Forbes

Using the figures we first examined—about $285,000 purchase price and $405 a week rent—the gross rental yield is about 7.4%. That is interesting because the rent is high relative to the purchase price.

1. MarketIs Forbes attractive enough to investigate?
2. PropertyDoes $405/week support a $285,000 purchase after real expenses?
3. Land6,070 m² creates possible value-add, but only if planning rules allow useful development.
4. RiskA flood overlay changes the question from “huge cheap block!” to “how much of this land is actually useful?”
5. VerifyGet a property-manager rental appraisal, insurance quote, council/rates information and planning advice before spending meaningful money.

The app's job is not to say “buy”. Its job is to turn a listing into a structured set of questions, show you where the money comes from, and identify what you need to verify next.

QUICK GLOSSARY

Words you will see in property investing

Demand to Supply Ratio

A supplied market score intended to show the balance between demand and available property.

Days on Market

How long properties generally take to sell. Falling or low days can indicate stronger buyer demand.

Stock on Market

How much property is listed for sale relative to the market. Lower or falling stock can indicate tighter supply.

Loan-to-Value Ratio

The loan amount divided by the property's value, expressed as a percentage.

Lenders Mortgage Insurance

Insurance protecting the lender when lending a high proportion of the property value.

Gross yield

Annual rent divided by purchase price before costs.

Net yield

Rental return after operating costs, usually before financing and tax depending on the chosen definition.

Vacancy allowance

A budget for periods when the property may have no tenant and therefore no rent.

Value-add

Something you can do to improve rent or value rather than relying only on the overall market to rise.
